# Fine-grained cooperation between two language models (decoding, reasoning steps, actions, effort), and what an API-only coding harness can use

Date of notes: 15 Nov 2026. Label key: [read] = seen in the paper's own full text (via a page fetch, which summarises the page with a small model, so numbers are as that fetch reported them); [abstract] = only abstract or search snippet; [secondary] = third-party account; [vendor] = provider documentation or blog (a vendor claim). Everything here is a preprint unless a venue is named. Search cost limits: I did NOT open the full text of Co-LLM, UniTE, DeePEn, PackLLM, GaC, contrastive decoding, speculative cascades (paper), FuseLLM, PASTE, AOSpec, Spex, "Reasoning that Travels" beyond its abstract page, or "Prompt-Induced Waste" (PDF text not extractable). ## 1. Decoding-time methods: what they buy and what access they need

### Takeaway
Every decoding-time method found needs per-token output distributions (or at least top-1 probability, or both models' hidden states) from at least one model, which hosted chat APIs do not give. The one that needs the least, Nudging, needs only the large model's top-1 token probability plus a completion-style endpoint that accepts a partial prefix. No hosted-API equivalent of speculative decoding with a user-chosen draft model exists; the nearest is OpenAI's Predicted Outputs, which is a user-supplied text guess.

### Cited Findings
- Speculative decoding (Leviathan, Kalman, Matias, Google; ICML 2023; arXiv 2211.17192): a small model drafts, the large model verifies in parallel; abstract reports 2X-3X acceleration on T5-XXL against the standard T5X implementation with identical outputs, no retraining. [abstract/secondary] — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.17192). Speedup depends on acceptance rate and draft cost; third-party listings of ~1.95x HumanEval and 2.82x GSM8K were seen only in an aggregator snippet [secondary, unverified]. Needs: both models' logits on the serving side (provider-side feature only).
- Speculative cascades (Narasimhan, Jitkrittum, Rawat, Kim, Gupta, Menon, Kumar; Google Research/DeepMind; ICLR 2025 "Faster Cascades via Speculative Decoding", arXiv 2405.19261; blog Sept 2025): drafter proposes tokens, target checks in parallel, but a flexible deferral rule (confidence threshold, confidence comparison, cost-benefit, top-k membership) replaces strict token matching, so the output is not guaranteed identical to the large model. Tested on Gemma and T5 on summarisation, translation, reasoning, coding, QA. The blog gives no numeric results in text; it claims better cost-quality trade-offs than cascades or speculative decoding alone. [vendor blog, read] — [Google Research blog](https://www.research.google/blog/speculative-cascades-a-hybrid-approach-for-smarter-faster-llm-inference); [paper listing](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.19261v2) (paper full text not opened). Needs: both models' logits, same serving stack.
- Proxy-tuning (Liu, Han, Wang, Tsvetkov, Choi, Smith; COLM 2024; arXiv 2401.08565): add the logit difference (small tuned minus small untuned) to a large base model's logits. Abstract-level results: closes 88% of the gap between Llama2-70B and 70B-Chat using 7B proxies (91% with 13B); also applied to GPT-3.5 for temporal adaptation. [secondary search summary of the abstract] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.08565). Needs the large model's output distribution; the Nudging paper states proxy-tuning needs top-100 probabilities overlapping across three models, "which most APIs don't support" [read, below].
- Nudging (Fei, Razeghi, Singh; arXiv 2410.09300 v4 June 2025; preprint): when the base model's top-1 probability falls below a threshold (gamma 0.3-0.4), a small aligned model writes a few "nudging" tokens (~10% of output). Models: Llama-2 70b/7b-chat, Gemma-2 27b/2b-it, OLMo 7b/1b-it. 12-13 benchmarks (GSM8K, ARC, CSQA, MMLU etc.), zero-shot averages [read]: Llama-2 base 33.8, 70b-chat 56.7, nudged 57.9; Gemma-2 base 12.4, 27b-it 74.4, nudged 70.3; OLMo base 28.9, 7b-it 39.2, nudged 40.8. GSM8K Gemma-2: base 6.7, nudged 74.6, 27b-it 85.4. Versus other methods (11 datasets, 200 samples each): average-ensemble 48.0/65.9/36.9, proxy-tuning 53.2/61.2/36.3, nudging 58.0/70.9/42.0. Time on 100 GSM8K samples via API-style access without prefix caching: nudging 286 s, ensemble 3,026 s, proxy-tuning 5,330 s. With prefix caching nudging 70b with 7b reaches 0.87x the throughput of 70b alone. Access: only the base model's top-1 logprob, works on completion APIs exposing it; works across model families (Gemma-2-27b nudged by Llama-2-7b-chat: GSM8K 6.7 to 65.3). Limits: heuristic does not tightly control which model produces tokens, fails on a constrained-writing example; English only; the targets are alignment/instruction-following of a base (non-chat) model, not coding agents. [read] — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09300v4) (second half of page not read).
- Co-LLM (Shen, Lang, Wang, Kim, Sontag, MIT; arXiv 2403.03870, 2024): small base model learns, via a latent-variable likelihood, per token whether to generate or call a larger assistant; reported to beat either model alone on instruction following, domain QA, reasoning; analysis shows template-fill behaviour. [abstract/secondary: numbers not read] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.03870). Needs training the base model plus hidden states/logits of both.
- Probability-combining ensembles (DeePEn, UniTE, GaC, PackLLM): only search-snippet descriptions seen. GaC maps all models' distributions to a union vocabulary; DeePEn averages in a shared relative-representation space; UniTE restricts to top-k tokens union; PackLLM weights models by prompt perplexity [secondary: search summary]. A later paper "When to Ensemble" (arXiv 2510.15346) says cost is dominated by aligning distributions over large vocabularies [secondary]. No numbers read. All need full or top-k token distributions from every model, hence local weights. — [When to Ensemble](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.15346); [Determine-Then-Ensemble](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03777v2).
- Contrastive decoding: not researched in this session (no sources opened). See Gaps.
- Hosted-API near-equivalents (vendor documentation):
  - OpenAI Predicted Outputs: "speed up API responses from Chat Completions when many of the output tokens are known ahead of time"; supported models listed as gpt-4o, gpt-4o-mini, gpt-4.1, gpt-4.1-mini, gpt-4.1-nano; "any tokens provided that are not part of the final completion are still charged at completion token rates"; unsupported: `n` > 1, `tools` ("Function calling is not currently supported"), audio, non-text modalities; example is refactoring a code file using the original file as prediction. [vendor, read] — [OpenAI docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/predicted-outputs.md). Azure's page reportedly warns that more rejected than accepted tokens can raise latency [secondary snippet]. One blog measure 1835 ms to 889 ms [secondary anecdote, with rejected tokens billed].
  - Provider "fast modes": not checked in this session; no page opened. Gap.

### Inferences
- For a harness, token-level methods are out unless it self-hosts. The only API-level analogue of "draft then verify" is Predicted Outputs for whole-file or large-block edits where the harness already knows most of the text; it cannot be combined with tool calls per the docs, which limits use inside a tool-call loop (it fits a plain "rewrite this file" request, not a tool-call edit).
- Nudging's idea (large model emits, small model intervenes only where the large one is uncertain) needs logprobs and a continuable prefix; many hosted chat APIs lack both.

### Gaps
- Co-LLM, UniTE, DeePEn, GaC, PackLLM numbers not read; contrastive decoding not covered; the speculative-cascade paper's numbers not read; provider fast modes not examined.

## 2. Token- and step-level routing inside one answer (small and large model)

### Takeaway
Papers report roughly 25-65% lower latency or cost against the large model, often at equal or slightly lower accuracy on math/science reasoning, using R1-distill pairs run locally. Coding evidence is thin: R2R and GlimpRouter include LiveCodeBench. All need local weights (hidden states or token entropy) and shared serving; none works through a hosted API.

### Cited Findings
- R2R, "Roads to Rome" (Fu, Ge et al., Tsinghua; NeurIPS 2025; arXiv 2505.21600 v2 Nov 2025): a 56M-parameter router, trained on 7.6M token-level labels (verified with Qwen2.5-72B; ~2.3 days on 8 A800), decides per token whether R1-Distill-1.5B or R1-32B generates; inputs are the small model's last hidden state, top-100 logits and token embedding. Greedy decoding, 32K max output. Table [read]: AIME 12% (1.5B), 57% (32B), 55% (R2R); LiveCodeBench 9%, 45%, 39%; GPQA 8%, 46%, 44%; average 10%, 50%, 46%. Large model used for ~11-15% of tokens (12.4% in the ablation); average activated parameters 5.6B; 2.8x wall-clock speedup over R1-32B (AIME 84.3 vs 30.5 tok/s; average latency 218 s vs 498 s). Limits: tuned mainly for greedy sampling; system-level optimisation still needed. Coding: 6-point drop on LiveCodeBench versus the 32B. [read] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21600v2) (last 54K characters unread).
- CITER (Zheng et al.; arXiv 2502.01976, v6; preprint): RL-trained token-level router (MLP over small model's last-token hidden state). Qwen2-1.5B/72B main pair; also Qwen2-7B and Llama3.1 8B/70B; benchmarks CommonsenseQA, ARC-Challenge, MMLU-Pro psychology, GSM8k, MATH; baselines RouteLLM, Co-LLM, speculative decoding. [read] Versus Co-LLM: "up to 27% fewer inference costs" at comparable accuracy or "up to 17%" higher accuracy at same cost (the intro says 30% and 25%, an internal inconsistency in the paper); versus RouteLLM up to 30% cost reduction or up to 12% accuracy gain. Cost is measured as data moved from GPU memory (KV cache), not wall-clock or share of tokens; latency compared only against speculative decoding in one setup (CITER 80.8% at ~4 s; speculative decoding 87.0% at 10.9 s). The paper says speculative decoding had so low an acceptance rate on math that it cost more than using the large model alone. No coding benchmark. Labels need ground-truth answers. — [arXiv HTML v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.01976v6).
- GlimpRouter (arXiv 2601.05110, Jan 2026; preprint): training-free, step-level; the small model writes only the first token of each reasoning step (steps split at blank lines); if that token's entropy exceeds a threshold, the large model writes the step. Qwen3-4B small, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B large; AIME24/25, GPQA-Diamond, LiveCodeBench v5/v6; Pass@1 over 4 runs. [read] LLM-only versus GlimpRouter: AIME24 57.50 to 60.83 (latency 197 s to 143 s); AIME25 46.67 to 51.67 (220 to 163); GPQA 61.62 to 64.02 (176 to 129); LCBv5 52.40 to 54.64 (219 to 160); LCBv6 46.86 to 48.29 (214 to 160), i.e. 25-27% lower latency. 26-37% of steps go to the large model. SLM-only: AIME24 48.33, LCBv5 47.90. With the 1.5B distill as small model, accuracy falls below LLM-only on every benchmark. Needs full-vocabulary first-token entropy (logits) and local vLLM. Authors' limitations: static global threshold; blank-line segmentation; small AIME sets, no variance reported. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.05110).
- Other 2026 token/step collaboration papers surfaced only as search snippets, no figures read: FusionRoute (arXiv 2601.05106), PyroDash (2607.20327), FutureBridge (2608.06819), TokenRouter serving system (summary in a Substack). [secondary] Treat as unverified leads.
- Speculative cascades (Section 1) is the Google-side analogue.

### Inferences
- Gains are largest where the small model is close enough to the large one (Qwen3-4B with R1-32B). A weak small model (R1-1.5B) loses accuracy in GlimpRouter. The coding data points (LiveCodeBench) show one method losing 6 points (R2R) and one gaining 1-2 points (GlimpRouter) versus the large model; samples are small and tasks are competitive programming, not repository editing.

### Gaps
- No token-level routing result on repository-level coding (SWE-bench style) was found. FusionRoute/PyroDash/FutureBridge/TokenRouter numbers not read.

## 3. Cooperation at the level of reasoning steps, through text

### Takeaway
Step-level schemes (SpecReason, Speculative Thinking, SplitReason) report 1.4-3x latency gains with equal or higher accuracy on math/science against the large model, but need two locally served models. Their text-only core (small model writes, large model scores a step with one generated token, no logits) is the part that could in principle run over APIs, though no one has shown that. Passing one model's reasoning to another is evidenced mostly by practitioner reports; one 2026 paper studies it systematically.

### Cited Findings
- SpecReason (Pan, Dai et al.; arXiv 2504.07891 v2; preprint): small model drafts a reasoning step, base model scores it with a single generated 0-9 utility token, rejected steps are regenerated by the base. Pairs: QwQ-32B and Skywork-OR1-Preview-32B with R1-1.5B and Zyphra ZR1-1.5B; AIME, MATH500, GPQA Diamond; pass@1 over 16 samples, 8192-token budget. [read] Latency 1.4-3.0x lower than vanilla base-model inference; accuracy +0.4 to +9.0 points over base; combined with speculative decoding a further 8.8-58.0% latency reduction over speculative decoding alone; 36-80% of steps accepted; 1.0-2.3x fewer thinking tokens; verification adds ~70 prefill tokens per step. Threshold knob (QwQ-32B + R1-1.5B subsets): MATH latency 35.7 s to 69.2 s while accuracy 59.4% to 63.7%; AIME 109.4 s to 261.9 s, 22.3% to 39.3%. R1-70B base: 1.5x on AIME (vs 1.9x for QwQ-32B). Needs: two models colocated under vLLM 0.8.2 with prefix caching; logits not required. No coding benchmarks. A caution in the paper: gains shrink when small model is much weaker; the 8192 budget truncates the baseline (AIME gap 16.2% at 4096 budget vs 4.7% at 8192), so some accuracy gain is lower verbosity under a token cap. — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07891v2). A follow-up ConfSpec (arXiv 2602.18447) exists [secondary, not read].
- Speculative Thinking (Yang, Yue, Chaudhary, Han; arXiv 2504.12329; preprint): small model generates; at step delimiters (blank lines, reflection words such as "wait") the large model takes over. Small DeepSeek-distilled Qwen-2.5 1.5B/7B, large 14B/32B; AIME, GPQA-Diamond, MATH500, AMC23. [read] 1.5B + 32B: AIME 25.6 to 32.2%, GPQA 33.8 to 41.9%, MATH500 83.2 to 89.4% (length 5439 to 4583), AMC23 75.0 to 80.0%; target modifies ~19-21% of tokens. 7B + 32B: little or no gain on MATH500 (92.8 to 93.0) and AMC23 (92.5 to 92.5); the standalone 32B scores 92.8 on MATH500 and 95.0 on AMC23. Non-reasoning Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct + 32B: MATH500 74.0 to 81.8, GPQA 33.8 to 48.0. Speed claim "170-200% faster" is a theoretical FLOP-based estimate, not wall-clock. Same model family assumed (shared KV cache). Note also that the improvement is measured against the small model alone, not against the large model; the large model alone is better on most columns. — [arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12329v1).
- SplitReason (Akhauri et al.; arXiv 2504.16379; preprint): 1.5B model fine-tuned (SFT on 18k traces annotated with deepseek-chat, then GRPO) to emit a "big model" tag at hard steps; offload models R1-Distill-Llama-8B/Qwen-14B/Qwen-32B (never fine-tuned). [read] AIME24: 1.5B 17.3%; 32B alone 64.0%; SplitR-32B 45.6% (+28.3 percentage points, not relative); 1.35% median offload per abstract, 5.54% observed in simulation. Latency 32B alone 3143 s vs SplitR-32B 918 s (~3.4x) in a pipelined simulation; "4-6x"/"over 5x" claims come from simulation; not measured end-to-end. Random 5-10% offloading also gained ~20 points. Needs training the small model and two local models. — [arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.16379v1).
- Reasoning from one model, answer from another:
  - DeepClaude (open-source project, getAsterisk): DeepSeek R1 produces the visible reasoning, Claude writes the answer; descriptions are third-party blogs and the README's claim that R1 is weaker at code generation than Claude 3.5 Sonnet is a project claim, not an evaluation. [secondary] — [DEV post](https://dev.to/pickuma/deepclaude-pairing-deepseek-r1-reasoning-with-claude-in-one-agent-loop-44i2). I found no peer-reviewed evaluation of DeepClaude itself.
  - Aider's R1-plans/Sonnet-edits result (64.0% Polyglot) is covered by the earlier survey and not repeated.
  - "Reasoning that Travels: Dissecting How Chain-of-Thought Transfers Across Models" (Cheng, Chen, Mondorf, Plank; arXiv 2605.28913, 27 May 2026; preprint): provider writes a chain of thought, receiver answers from growing prefixes. [abstract only] Full traces often transfer; on AIME force-answer transfer depends largely on whether the explicit answer is in the prefix; on MMLU-Pro on receiver competence; in free-generation mode partial traces improve performance; receiver agreement can signal early stopping. Model names and numbers not on the page I read. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28913).
  - A censorship audit says R1's chain of thought is passed to the base model DeepSeek-V3 to produce the answer in DeepSeek's own pipeline; this is the audit's description [secondary] — [arXiv 2506.12349](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.12349).
  - Practical note on API access: Anthropic's docs state thinking blocks from prior turns count as input and for some models are preserved and must be passed back unmodified [vendor, read] ([Steering thinking](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/thinking-steering-and-cost) page; "pass them back unmodified"). Text-form reasoning from another provider (DeepSeek's `reasoning_content`) can be pasted as plain text into another model's prompt, as practitioner posts describe [secondary]; no measured gain on coding agents found.

### Inferences
- The most transferable idea is the SpecReason verifier: a one-token score by the strong model over a weak model's step. In a harness it maps to "weak model proposes the next tool call or edit, strong model approves with a short reply", which is a whole-message pattern (already in the team's advisor/reviewer family), but the paper shows the step must be cheap to check (about 70 prefill tokens, 1-2 decoded tokens) relative to regeneration. Via APIs the check costs a full request round trip, which changes the economics; not measured anywhere I found.

### Gaps
- No coding-task results for SpecReason, Speculative Thinking or SplitReason. No controlled measure of a "reasoner + answerer" pair against the answerer alone on coding was found besides Aider's (excluded). No wall-clock measures for SplitReason.

## 4. Speculation for agents

### Takeaway
Speculative Actions (Columbia, ICLR 2026) shows about 20% end-to-end latency saving with up to 55% next-action prediction accuracy, using LLM speculators via ordinary APIs; the cost is extra tokens, not quantified in text. Systems work on speculative tool execution reports 14-48% savings on SWE-bench-like traces, but I could only confirm these from secondary snippets.

### Cited Findings
- Speculative Actions: A Lossless Framework for Faster Agentic Systems (Ye et al., Columbia; arXiv 2510.04371, 5 Oct 2025; ICLR 2026 as "...Faster AI Agents"). A fast speculator guesses the actor's next action; correct guesses are committed early. [read]: chess (actor GPT-5 high reasoning; speculator GPT-5 low reasoning) 19.5% time saved, 54.7% prediction accuracy, 3 predictions; tau-bench retail 22-38% API prediction accuracy (multi-model speculators beat single); HotpotQA up to 46% (top-3, strict matching); OS tuning (lossy) p95 latency 102.97 ms untuned, 54.00 actor-only, 37.93 with speculator, convergence about 10-15 s vs ~200 s. Cost: OS task at 200 s: actor-only 63,376 tokens (2.18 cents), speculator-only 2,099,894 tokens (21.5 cents), actor+speculator 607,877 tokens (7.83 cents), i.e. about 3.6x the actor-only cost for faster convergence. Theoretical bound: ideal ~50% reduction. Side-effect safeguards (semantic guards, safety envelopes for idempotent actions, repair paths) are proposed but not experimentally tested; irreversible actions flagged as risky. Wrong-guess token cost for chess/e-commerce is only in figures not text. Evaluated via API models (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Flash). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04371v1); [ICLR page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10009726).
- PASTE, "Parallelizing Tool Execution and LLM Generation for Low-Latency Agent Serving" (Sui et al.; arXiv 2603.18897 v3; cs.DC): PDF text was unreadable, so only title, authors, and date are confirmed. A wiki page reports a 48.5% reduction in average task completion time on SWE-bench and MetaGPT workloads [secondary, unverified]. AOSpec (arXiv 2608.00881): snippet says 18.9% latency savings on SWE-bench for a model trained on Terminal-Bench traces versus 4.2% for the strongest baseline [secondary snippet]. Spex, an open-source Codex speculation layer, claims 13.9% lower trace wall time at identical resolution on 42 SWE-bench Verified instances [project README claim, secondary]. "Optimizing Agentic LM Inference via Speculative Tool Calls" (arXiv 2512.15834) title only. These come from different setups and are not comparable.
- Predicted edits: OpenAI Predicted Outputs (Section 1) is the only hosted feature; tool-calling incompatibility is documented.

### Inferences
- For a harness, speculation is safest for read-only tools (file reads, grep, listing), where a wrong guess wastes only compute. For writes or shell commands, rollback is needed. The measured savings are 14-20% from independent-looking sources, with one 48.5% claim unverified.

### Gaps
- PASTE and AOSpec papers not read. No measure of wrong-guess cost in a coding harness. Speculator accuracy on edit actions not found.

## 5. Reasoning effort as a second "model", adaptive thinking, and caching

### Takeaway
Providers now expose effort controls, and Anthropic documents that changing effort or thinking configuration invalidates message-level cache breakpoints, but offers two workarounds: per-message effort (beta) and per-message prompt steering, both said to keep the cache. OpenAI documents a `configuration_update` input item to change effort without breaking the prefix. Controlled evidence on high-effort planning plus low-effort execution is thin.

### Cited Findings
- Anthropic [vendor, read]: "The resolved effort value is rendered into the prompt, so changing it between requests can invalidate cache breakpoints. ... Setting `effort` explicitly to the model's default is equivalent to omitting it and does not break the cache." Its worked example on Claude Opus 5.5 shows effort medium (default) to low: second request cache_read 3546 tokens, third request cache_creation 3546 and cache_read 0. It recommends per-message effort (beta) or per-message prompting ("Please think hard before responding." / "Answer directly without deliberating.") since both "leave earlier cache breakpoints intact"; an agent harness "can append the encouraging phrase on planning steps and the discouraging phrase on routine confirmations". Caveat in the docs: the discouraging phrase does not reliably reduce thinking on Claude Haiku 5.5 and Claude Fable 5.2 (and Sonnet 5.5 system-prompt instructions). In a tool loop "the first request after new user input typically carries most of the reasoning, and follow-up requests that only process tool results can skip thinking". — [Steering thinking](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/thinking-steering-and-cost). Older manual mode: changing `budget_tokens` invalidates message-level breakpoints (example: 4,000 to 8,000 produced a full re-creation); switching mode also invalidates; whether system/tool breakpoints survive "depends on where the model renders the configuration". Extended thinking (manual budget) is deprecated; 4.7 and later reject it with a 400. — [Extended thinking](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/extended-thinking). Per-message effort page itself not opened.
- OpenAI [vendor, read]: "append a `configuration_update` input item to change reasoning effort between responses" with "{"type": "configuration_update", "reasoning": {"effort": "high"}}"; "changing that setting can rewrite instructions in the hidden system instructions"; "Cache reuse requires the entire rendered prefix to match"; cached reads cost 0.1x input on most listed models, 24h/in-memory retention options, "discounted up to 95%". The exact claim that a configuration update preserves the earlier prefix is my reading; the fetch gave only the quoted fragments. A diagnostics page lists a `reasoning_effort_changed` miss reason per a search summary [secondary, not opened]. — [Prompt caching](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching.md).
- Google (Gemini): search results show implicit caching on by default for 2.5 and newer, per-model minimum token thresholds, and a Gemini 3 `thinking_level` parameter replacing integer `thinking_budget` [secondary: search summaries]. A developer-forum thread reports implicit caching failing when a thinking budget is set [forum report, unconfirmed]. I found no official statement on how changing thinking config affects caches. Not verified.
- DeepSeek: prefix-based disk caching on by default, `prompt_cache_hit_tokens` reported [secondary: search summary of DeepSeek's page]; nothing found on reasoning-setting changes.
- Evidence on effort split: no controlled paper found. "Prompt-Induced Waste in Coding Agents: Reasoning, Effort, Harness Design, and End-to-End Cost" (Weinberger, Hozez; arXiv 2608.01347 v6) exists, but the PDF text was unreadable; only the introduction claim (more effort helps hard tasks but can add computation without better solutions) came via a search snippet. A news write-up cites a July 2026 study where high to xhigh lifted first-try perfect runs from 28% to 89% on one workload [secondary; original not found]. Practitioner sources describe a "reasoning sandwich" (heavy reasoning for planning and verification, light for execution) and Cursor's tiered design [secondary, not evaluated].

### Inferences
- With Anthropic, within one conversation, an effort change is a cache miss unless the per-message effort beta is available for the model; a prompt-level steer is cheaper but not reliable on some models. With OpenAI a documented mechanism exists. For Google and DeepSeek the harness would have to measure cache-hit tokens itself.

### Gaps
- Per-message effort feature page, Google and DeepSeek official docs on effort/thinking changes, and the July 2026 study not read.

## 6. Methods needing weights (for completeness)

- Model merging (weight averaging, task arithmetic, TIES and similar): combines parameters of same-architecture fine-tunes into one model; no sources opened in this session. [no source]
- Knowledge fusion, FuseLLM (Wan et al.; ICLR 2024; arXiv 2401.10491): transfers the generative distributions of several source LLMs (Llama-2, MPT, OpenLLaMA) into one target LLM by lightweight continual training; reports gains in reasoning, commonsense and code generation; contrasted with ensembles (need parallel deployment) and weight merging (needs identical architectures). [abstract/secondary] — [ICLR page](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/4fd5cfd2e31bebbccfa5ffa354c04bdc-Abstract-Conference.html).
- Mixing adapters (for example LoraHub): composes LoRA adapters; searched but no source surfaced. [no source]

## 7. Fine-grained versus coarse mixing of the same two models

### Takeaway
I found few direct comparisons. The ones I read compare token/step routing with query-level routing or random routing, not with plain per-task assignment.

### Cited Findings
- CITER [read]: token-level routing reaches up to 30% lower cost than RouteLLM (query-level routing) at similar accuracy, or up to 12% higher accuracy at equal cost. Its own figures are internally inconsistent in places (see Section 2). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.01976v6).
- GlimpRouter [read] compares against a random baseline and the SpecReason/RSD/SpecCoT step-level baselines, but not whole-task routing. SplitReason [read]: random offloading of 5-10% of steps recovered about 20 points of accuracy, so most of the gain comes from using the large model at all and the learned choice adds the rest; the paper does not separate them with per-task routing.
- R2R: compares to R1-7B/R1-14B (different model sizes) and a few routers; per-task routing between the same two models not seen in what I read.

### Gaps
- No same-two-models head-to-head of token/step mixing vs per-task or per-request mixing in a coding setting found.

## Final lists

### (1) Methods a coding harness with API access only could actually use, with evidence
1. Per-message steering or per-message effort for plan/execute effort splits: Anthropic documents it as cache-preserving [vendor, read]; evidence that the split improves quality or cost is only practitioner-level.
2. OpenAI `configuration_update` to change reasoning effort mid-conversation [vendor, read, quoted fragments]; verify actual cache hit rates in the harness's own usage fields.
3. Predicted Outputs for large-block code rewrites where the original file is the prediction [vendor, read]; billed for rejected tokens, no tool calling, listed models only. Latency evidence is anecdotal [secondary].
4. Speculative Actions / speculative read-only tool execution: Columbia paper used API models, up to 55% next-action accuracy and about 20% latency saving [read], extra tokens cost (3.6x in the OS task). SWE-bench-oriented systems claim 14-48% [secondary, unverified]. Restrict to idempotent tools.
5. Step-level text-only verification (the SpecReason pattern: strong model scores a weak model's step with a few tokens): demonstrated only on local models with math/science; an API version is an inference, not a measured result.
6. Reasoning-from-one-model, answer-from-another: usable via plain text; measured on coding only by Aider (excluded here); 2026 transfer study suggests gains partly come from answer leakage in the trace [abstract].

### (2) Methods needing access the harness lacks, or not surviving sober evaluation
- Token-level methods needing logits or hidden states: speculative decoding, speculative cascades, proxy-tuning, Co-LLM, CITER, R2R, GlimpRouter, DeePEn, UniTE, GaC, PackLLM. Nudging needs only top-1 logprob plus prefix continuation, which hosted chat APIs generally do not provide.
- Weight-level: merging, FuseLLM, adapter mixing.
- SplitReason: needs fine-tuning, latency from simulation. Speculative Thinking: speed figures theoretical, gains measured against the weak model and zero or near zero over a 7B small model.
- R2R lost 6 points on LiveCodeBench versus the 32B; CITER's internal inconsistencies and cost metric limit trust.

### (3) Open questions
- Does a one-token strong-model approval of a weak model's tool call pay off once each check is a full API request (and its cache effect)?
- How much of step-routing gain survives on repository-level coding rather than AIME/GPQA/LiveCodeBench?
- Are Anthropic's per-message effort and OpenAI's configuration_update truly cache-preserving in practice, and what are the Google and DeepSeek behaviours?
- Wrong-guess cost of speculative edits and shell commands in real harnesses (PASTE/AOSpec/Spex numbers need primary-source checking).
- Direct comparison of per-token/step mixing with per-task routing of the same two models.
- Not covered: contrastive decoding, provider fast modes, merging/adapter sources, UniTE/DeePEn/GaC/PackLLM numbers.

### A. Provider documentation: changing effort or thinking settings mid-conversation and the prompt cache

**Anthropic [vendor, read]**
- Feature name and status: "Per-message effort", page title "Per-message effort", description "Use a system message to change the effort level partway through a conversation and keep the prompt cache." Status `beta`; beta header `mid-conversation-output-config-2026-07-01`. Supported models listed in the page's metadata: claude-fable-5-2, claude-mythos-5-2, claude-fable-5-1, claude-mythos-5-1, claude-opus-5-5, claude-opus-5, claude-sonnet-5-5, claude-haiku-5-5. Platforms: Claude API plus Bedrock, Claude Platform on AWS, Google Cloud, Foundry (all beta). Not available in Managed Agents. — [Per-message effort](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/effort-per-message)
- Mechanism: "A per-message effort change sets a new effort level for later turns of a conversation without changing the request's top-level value, so the cached prefix still matches." Steps: "Add a `role: "system"` message to `messages`, with empty `content` and the new effort level in `output_config.effort`", send the beta header, "Leave the top-level value unchanged." Example sets top-level `high` and drops to `low` for a follow-up.
- Timing: "Directly after a `user` turn with new input: Claude's reply to that turn uses the new level. In any other position ... the new level starts at the next `user` turn with new input. A `user` turn that holds only `tool_result` blocks doesn't count as new input." This matters for harnesses: a tool-result-only turn does not pick up the new level.
- Placement rule: "Put the message after everything you've already sent, and keep it in place on later requests. Don't insert it before turns you've already sent, and don't move or remove it later." On models with preserved thinking, such changes invalidate thinking blocks in later assistant turns.
- Errors: without the header a per-message `output_config` returns 400; models without support (named: Claude Fable 5) return 400; "On Claude Sonnet 5.5 with `thinking: {"type": "between_tools"}`, and on Claude Haiku 5.5 with `thinking: {"type": "disabled"}`, you can't change the effort level mid-conversation"; use adaptive thinking.
- Cache comparison: "Prefer per-message effort over changing the top-level value between requests. A per-message change leaves everything before it unchanged, so the cached prefix still matches. A top-level change can invalidate all or part of the cache. On Claude Fable 5.1, a top-level change also steers the model less reliably."
- Top-level change, from the Steering thinking page (first pass, [read]): "The resolved effort value is rendered into the prompt, so changing it between requests can invalidate cache breakpoints." Worked example with Opus 5.5: cache_read 3546 on the unchanged second request, cache_creation 3546 and cache_read 0 after medium to low. Extended-thinking page: changing `budget_tokens` between requests "invalidates cache breakpoints"; "Message-level breakpoints always miss after a budget change; whether tool and system-prompt breakpoints miss too depends on where the model renders the configuration." Manual `budget_tokens` mode is deprecated; 4.7 and later reject it.
- Untested by me: whether the cache actually survives in a real harness loop; I did not run any calls.
- Related fast-mode cache rule (also vendor, read): "Falling back from fast to standard speed results in a prompt cache miss. Requests at different speeds do not share cached prefixes." — [Fast mode](https://platform.claude.com/docs/build-with-claude/fast-mode)

**OpenAI [vendor, read]**
- Prompt caching guide (first pass): "append a `configuration_update` input item to change reasoning effort between responses"; "changing that setting can rewrite instructions in the hidden system instructions"; "Cache reuse requires the entire rendered prefix to match." Example item: `{"type": "configuration_update", "reasoning": {"effort": "high"}}`.
- Diagnostics page, [read], quotes as returned by the fetch: "On supported GPT-6 and later models, append a `configuration_update` input item to" change reasoning effort, "while preserving the earlier cached prefix." The miss reason `reasoning_effort_changed` is defined as "The reasoning effort changed." The fetch tool paraphrased the first sentence with a gap ("to" then a missing clause), so the exact full sentence should be confirmed on the page. — [Diagnostics](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching/diagnostics); [Prompt caching](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching.md)

**Google [vendor, read]**

For Gemini 4 agent loops: "Gemini caches the full previous request only if the new request adds at least 2,050 tokens." "Model output, thought, and function results all count toward this total." ### B. "Reasoning that Travels" (arXiv 2605.28913), full text read
- Authors Cheng, Chen, Mondorf, Plank; submitted 27 May 2026; preprint. [read, HTML] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28913)

### C. Controlled evidence on effort allocation and adaptive vs fixed effort (coding/agent)
- "What Effort Level Should I Choose? On the Economics of Reasoning Effort in LLM Agents" (Sun, Kwon, Hasan, Zhu, Zhou, Bianchi; arXiv 2610.34491 v1, 25 Oct 2026; preprint). [read, HTML] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.34491)
  - Eight models (including GPT-5.6-sol/terra/luna and Claude Sonnet 5, plus open-weight GLM-5.2, Kimi K3, DeepSeek-V4-Flash); AIME 2026, GPQA Diamond, Terminal-Bench 2.1 (89 tasks), SWE-Bench Pro (44-instance pilot).
  - Leakage: "GPT-5.6-sol reads the reference-fix commit on 15.9% of tasks at low, compared with 75.0% at xhigh"; its xhigh SWE-Bench Pro solve rate falls from 77.3% (original) to 59.1% (corrected environment, git history removed, network restricted). So part of higher-effort "gains" on the original environment came from reading the fix.
  - Cost: on always-solved SWE-Bench Pro tasks GPT-5.6-sol cost "rises from $0.27 to $1.24" (4.54x); "highest effort level costing up to 42x" the lowest on easy tasks (the fetch could not say which model or benchmark).
  - Escalation ("naive laddering": start low, escalate to top effort only after a verified failure) "matches or improves success over fixed top effort across all evaluated models and benchmarks"; median cost saving vs highest effort 25.3% (AIME), 41.5% (GPQA), 15.5% (corrected SWE-Bench Pro). Terminal-Bench: GPT-5.6-sol 83.0% to 88.6% success with 17.4% lower cost; Kimi K3 73.0% to 84.3% with 30.5% lower cost.
  - Does not test plan-high/execute-low. Limits stated: each agentic task run once per effort level; laddering needs a reliable external correctness check; 44-instance SWE-Bench Pro sample; wage-based waiting cost is assumed.
- "Prompt-Induced Waste in Coding Agents: Reasoning, Effort, Harness Design, and End-to-End Cost" (Weinberger, Hozez; arXiv 2608.01347 v6; preprint; HTML opened, first ~100K of 157K characters read). [read, partial]
  - "Six open-weight reasoning models (≥500B-class, served by one provider) and Claude Sonnet 5 were evaluated on 24 deterministic coding tasks"; "SWE-bench campaign contains 150 paired model-task-effort cells and manipulates effort level rather than prompt wording."
  - Effort-control arm, Sonnet 5, DeepSeek Harness, n=15 per arm, "15/15 success in every arm": mean billed cost per run baseline $0.2753, effort-control $0.0696, full stack $0.0490; "Under dsh the effort arm reduced cost by about 75% and the full stack by about 82%"; under Claude Code "about 19%" and "about 27%". So the effort lever's saving depends strongly on the harness. All arms succeed on easy tasks, so no quality information.
  - Adaptive control: "A follow-up adaptive-compute campaign could not create baseline failures even with purpose-built deceptive tasks"; "the controller itself is a proposed follow-up and is not evaluated". No phase-specific effort result in the part read.
- Secondary only (not opened): a blog reports an Anthropic SWE-bench Pro sweep where a mixed-effort policy reached about 97% at about $0.17 per task vs 95.3% at $0.29 all-high [secondary, vendor figure via blog, unverified, single run]; a summary of the 2025 Berkeley "Danger of Overthinking" work on OpenHands reports o1 at high effort 29.1% resolved at $1,400 vs low effort 21.0% at $400 [secondary]. I did not locate or read the originals.
- Anthropic documentation claim (vendor, read): "In a tool-use loop, the first request after new user input typically carries most of the reasoning, and follow-up requests that only process tool results can skip thinking, including at `xhigh` and `max`." and the harness suggestion to "append the encouraging phrase on planning steps and the discouraging phrase on routine confirmations".
- Conclusion: still no controlled plan-high/execute-low study. The best evidence is escalation-after-verified-failure beating fixed top effort in cost without losing success, on small samples.

### D. Speculation for agents: figures now checked in HTML
- PASTE (Sui, Zhao, Ma, He, Wang, Li, Xu, Chen, Yang; arXiv 2603.18897; preprint) [read, HTML]. Corrects first pass: the 48.5% figure from a wiki was wrong. Abstract: "reduces average task completion time by 43.5%" and "lowers observed tool latency by 1.8x". Body: "PASTE reduces average latency by up to 43.5%, with p99 tail latency improving by up to 55.4%"; tool latency average down up to 55.2%; tool-side speedup 1.71x over ORION and 1.83x over SpecFaaS. Benchmarks DeepResearchBench, SWE-bench, ScholarQA; agents VirtualLab, Qwen Deep Research, gemini-cli; models Qwen-DeepResearch-30B and Qwen3-30B-A3B served with vLLM on 32 A100s. Predictor: "up to 27.8% Top-1 accuracy, 43.9% Top-3 recall, and 93.8% overall hit rate" (the hit rate is high partly because several predictions are issued per call). Cost of wrong guesses: "At a moderate speculation budget, PASTE reduces tool execution latency by 48%" using "1-3 otherwise idle CPU cores and 250 MB of additional memory"; no wasted-work percentage given. Side effects: "detects 602 potentially side-effecting speculative actions among over 20,000" and prevented them from committing; "No task produces a different final result compared with the baselines"; "Tools with side effects require an explicit safe-speculation policy or a non-mutating transformed execution." This is tool-execution speculation (no extra LLM calls), so its costs are CPU and memory, not tokens. Final ~1K characters of the page unread. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18897)
- AOSpec (Chen, Guo, Luk, Fan; arXiv 2608.00881, 1 Aug 2026; preprint) [abstract]. Corrects first pass: "reducing mean end-to-end latency by 11.8-32.5% and p99 latency by up to 42.8%" across "Terminal-Bench serving settings spanning four harnesses, five actor models, and five serving speeds"; the authors say its observation model transfers to SWE-bench Verified without retraining. The 18.9% SWE-bench figure from the first pass is secondary and was not verified on the abstract page. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00881)
- Spex (open-source repo cchang3906/spex; project README, not peer reviewed) [read, README]: "13.9 percent less trace wall time at identical resolution" across 84 sealed runs; "38 of 42 solved in both arms"; "88 of 110 verification calls" served from speculation (~80%). Mechanism: "The client then runs likely commands in a shadow queue with a budget of 2 slots and caches the output upon completion"; the "verifier resolver supplies the exact command and arguments from repository conventions"; agents are told to use a custom tool for tests, lint, typecheck; "wrong guesses are never shown to the model"; "every edit fences stale results." So it speculates only verification commands (test/lint/typecheck), a client-side technique usable with any API. Small sample (42 instances, one project's own claim). — [Spex README](https://github.com/cchang3906/spex)
