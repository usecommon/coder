# Second, independent check of 23 papers

*15 November 2026. After the nine sets of notes in this folder were written, the papers behind the headline findings were fetched again by a separate reader that had not seen the notes, and asked to quote the sentences that carry the figures. The fetch tool returns a model-made extract of each page, so this is a second extraction, not a reading of the raw page. Where the two extractions agree the figure is marked "confirmed". Where they differ the difference is stated, and the report uses the checked version.*

All 23 pages exist and are the papers the notes describe.

## Routing, hand-over and effort

| Paper | Checked | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **LLMRouterBench** (Hao Li and 11 others, [2601.07206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07206), 12 January 2026) | Abstract and full text | Confirmed. "top routing methods achieve up to a 4% average accuracy gain over the Best Single model"; "up to a 31.7% cost reduction while matching Best Single performance"; OpenRouter "yields the smallest (indeed, negative) performance improvement (-24.7%) relative to the Best Single model". Table rows (through a summary): small-model pool, best single 68.01, best router 71.94, perfect router 91.64; flagship pool, best single (GPT-5) 65.96, best router 68.58, perfect router 85.66, OpenRouter 49.67 |
| **TwinRouterBench** (Pei Yang and 17 others, [2605.18859](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18859)) | Abstract and full text (v5) | Confirmed, with one correction: first submitted **14 May 2026** (the notes say 8 May); v5 on 24 October 2026. Cost $25.66 against $54.73 (53.1% lower) at 75 of 100 resolved against 74 of 100 for Opus 4.6 alone. The 100 tasks are 18 from Astropy and 82 from Django, "not a random sample of the 500-case suite". Verbatim: "a single-template LLM router predicts high for only 7 of 147 verified-high SWE-bench steps and fails every SWE trajectory" |
| **Agent-as-a-Router** (ACRouter and CodeRouterBench; Pengfei Zhou and 10 others, [2606.22902](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22902), 22 June 2026) | Abstract and full text (v3) | In-distribution figures confirmed: router 49.98, perfect router 57.00, always Opus 4.6 43.83. **Out-of-distribution figures differ from the first-pass notes.** On the 176 agentic tasks the router resolves 62.50%, always Opus 4.6 57.14%, the perfect router 75.89%, and **GPT-5.4 alone 75.00%**, above the router. The "about 20" in the notes refers to other trained routers, not to this one |
| **The Handoff Tax** (Roy Ganz, Mor Shpigel Nacson, Adi Kalyanpur, Ron Litman, [2608.24358](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24358), 25 August 2026) | Abstract and full text | Confirmed. SWE-bench Verified, 500 tasks, mini-swe-agent. Share of the gap between the cheap and the strong model that is recovered: with the full transcript 47% (Claude Haiku 4.5 to Opus 4.7) and 36% (GPT-5.6 Luna to Sol); with the transcript dropped and the edits kept 64% and 84%; a fresh start by the strong model 100% by construction. Mean cost per task, Claude pair then GPT pair: cheap alone $0.40 / $0.06; strong alone $0.72 / $0.47; full transcript $1.61 / $0.36; transcript dropped $0.81 / $0.50; abort and fresh start $0.90 / $0.51 |
| **What Effort Level Should I Choose?** (Kaiser Sun and 5 others, [2610.34491](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34491), 25 October 2026) | Abstract and full text | Confirmed. Starting at the lowest effort and escalating "only after a verified failure" matches or improves on fixed top effort; median saving 25.3% (AIME), 41.5% (GPQA), 15.5% (corrected SWE-Bench Pro, a 44-task pilot). Terminal-Bench 2.1 (89 tasks): GPT-5.6 Sol 83.0% to 88.6% with cost down 17.4%; Claude Sonnet 5 76.1% to 83.0% with cost down 8.7%; Kimi K3 73.0% to 84.3% with cost down 30.5%; the other four models gained success at higher cost. The paper notes that this "requires a reliable external correctness check" |

## Knowing that a run is failing

| Paper | Checked | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Hard Issue, Weak Model, or Bad Run?** (Xingyue Shi, Wesley Assunção, [2611.08285](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08285), 5 November 2026) | Abstract and full text | Confirmed. Issue difficulty alone "reaches 0.92 AUROC, whereas trajectory predictors reach about 0.62"; what the run adds beyond difficulty and model ability is "statistically equivalent to zero". Escalating every failure to one second model: +6.91 points, 95% interval −4.67 to +9.15 |
| **Continue or Restart?** (Srinivasan, Li, Menzies, Assunção, [2610.35971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35971), 26 October 2026) | Abstract | Confirmed in substance, **with a correction to the count**: 147,002 traces; within-task AUC 0.50 (0.48 to 0.51); continuing beat both restart policies "across all four settings at the 75k cap (and 216 of 224 tested budget-threshold configurations overall)". The notes' "56 of 56" was not found |
| **Investigating Test Overfitting on SWE-bench** (Ahmed, Ganhotra, Shinnar, Hirzel, IBM, [2511.16858](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16858), 20 November 2025, v3 3 April 2026) | Full text | Confirmed. Share of patches that pass the generated tests and fail the developers' or the regression tests: 21.8% (Claude 3.7 Sonnet, 50 of 229) and 33.0% (GPT-4o, 58 of 176); with a refinement loop 25.5% and 35.9%. Patches from Agentless, tests from e-Otter++, TDD-Bench Verified |
| **AI Agents That Matter** (Kapoor, Stroebl, Siegel, Nadgir, Narayanan, [2407.01502](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01502), 1 July 2024) | Full text | Confirmed for retry (92.0%, $2.51), warming (93.2%, $2.45), escalation (85.0%, $0.27), LATS with GPT-4 (88.0%, $134.50), Reflexion (87.8%, $3.90). Verbatim: "'State-of-the-art' agent architectures for HumanEval do not outperform simple baselines." **The cost of the LDB rows differed between the two extractions** and should be read from the paper's table before it is quoted |

## Automatic design, roles and context files

| Paper | Checked | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **The Illusion of Multi-Agent Advantage** (Jwalapuram and 9 others, [2606.13003](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13003), 11 June 2026) | Abstract and full text (v2) | Confirmed: "automatic MAS consistently underperform CoT-SC despite being up to 10x more expensive." The baseline is a majority vote over five samples. SWE-Bench Lite with GPT-5: 57.09 for the vote, 55.97 for the best designed system (DyLAN), 27 to 46 for the other five. **Two qualifications** from the table (read through a summary): with Gemini 2.5 Pro one designed system scored above the vote (27.60 against 26.50); and on SWE-Bench Lite several designed systems cost less than the vote, so "up to ten times the cost" is not true of every benchmark |
| **Can AI Models Direct Each Other?** (Rui Liu, one author, [2603.26458](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26458), 27 March 2026) | Abstract and full text | Confirmed. SWE-bench Lite, 200 tasks: a Sonnet 4.6 manager with a GPT-5-mini worker 62% (124); Sonnet 4.6 alone 60% (120); GPT-5-mini alone 51% (101). On a 50-task subset a weak manager with a weak worker scored 42% against 44% for the weak model alone. Also in the abstract: "a minimal review-only loop adds just 2pp over the baseline, while structured exploration and planning add 11pp" |
| **Evaluating AGENTS.md** (Gloaguen and 4 others, ETH Zurich, [2602.11988](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11988), 12 February 2026, v3 29 September 2026) | Abstract and full text (v3) | Main conclusion confirmed: "providing context files does not generally improve task success rates", while "increasing inference cost by over 20% on average". In v3: model-written files lower the average resolution rate by 0.5% on SWE-bench tasks and 2% on the authors' own benchmark, with drops in 5 of 8 settings; developer-written files "outperform LLM-generated ones by a significant margin of 7% on average" (p = 0.038) and do not differ significantly from no file (p = 0.21). **The per-model figures in the first-pass notes came from an unversioned page and were not re-confirmed** |
| **SkillsBench** ([2602.12670](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12670), 13 February 2026) | Search summary of the abstract | Confirmed at abstract level: curated skills +16.2 points on average, from +4.5 (software engineering) to +51.9 (healthcare); self-written skills −1.3 points. The abstract says 86 tasks and the body 84 |

## Teams and gatekeepers

| Paper | Checked | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Scaling Inference-Time Compute with Teams of Coding Agents** (Samway and 5 others, Cohere, [2610.17471](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17471), 12 October 2026) | Abstract | Confirmed. "On ProgramBench, teams outperform solo agents but cost three to seven times as much." A lead that delegates only planning "matches the reward" of full delegation "at half the cost". "Cheap models pay off as planners but not as implementers: a strong lead with cheap planners recovers 47.5% of the reward gain of an all-strong team for only 13.9% of its additional cost, whereas cheap implementers lower reward." Implementers and verifiers "plateau beyond four workers". A mixed team of 17 agents comes within 1.4 points of a strong team of 5 at 70% lower cost. **The abstract gives no size for the gain of a team over one agent**; the "about 10 points" in the notes comes from the company's post |
| **TeamBench** (Yubin Kim and 11 others, [2605.07073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07073), 8 May 2026) | Abstract | Confirmed. "Verifiers approve 49% of submissions that fail the deterministic grader, and removing the verifier improves mean partial score in the ablation." "Teams benefit when single agents struggle, but hurt when single agents already perform well." |
| **Measuring the Permission Gate** (Zimo Ji and 5 others, [2604.04978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04978), 4 April 2026, v3 22 October 2026) | Abstract | Confirmed. Miss rate 81.0% (interval 73.8 to 87.4) over 253 state-changing actions; 36.8% of them were file edits the classifier never sees; on the 160 it did evaluate, miss rate 70.3% and false-alarm rate 31.9%. The vendor's own figures were 0.4% false alarms and 17% misses |
| **SWE-Verifier** (Kaijian Zou and 7 others, [2610.30284](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30284), 21 October 2026) | Abstract | Confirmed: 75.47% to 77.20% on MiniMax-M2.7 at 1.09 times the cost; also 72.60% to 74.27% on DeepSeek-V4-Flash at 1.13 times the cost; it checks fewer than 4% of steps. The verifier is a trained 4B model |
| **Deep Agentic Search for Repository-Level Code Question Answering** (Oskooei and 7 others, [2608.01507](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01507), 2 August 2026) | Abstract | Confirmed: semantic search 65.2% against 46.2% for delegated exploration, at less than half the cost per correct answer; 41.8% of the failures occurred at the hand-off between planner and sub-agent |
| **Prompt-Induced Waste in Coding Agents** (Weinberger, Hozez, [2608.01347](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01347), 2 August 2026, v6 10 September 2026) | Abstract | The paper exists; its abstract has no figures, so the notes' figures were not re-confirmed |

## Search and repeated attempts

| Paper | Checked | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Coding Agents Have Converged** (Fengshuo Liu and 4 others, [2609.17394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17394), 15 September 2026) | Abstract | Confirmed. On SWE-bench Verified the two leading entries each resolve 396 of 500; "the top ten share 285 successes and 51 failures, leaving 164 instances that distinguish their outcomes"; the range between scaffolds on one model reaches 29.8 points against an 8.8-point spread among the top thirty; paired tests separate none of the 29 adjacent pairs in the top thirty |
| **The Limits of Inference Scaling Through Resampling** (Stroebl, Kapoor, Narayanan, [2411.17501](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17501), 26 November 2024, v3 26 March 2026) | Abstract | Confirmed. With a checker that sometimes passes wrong answers, "no amount of inference scaling of weaker models can enable them to match the single-sample accuracy of a sufficiently strong model"; "optimal sampling attempts are often fewer than 10" |
| **TUMIX** (Yongchao Chen and 8 others, Google, [2510.01279](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01279), 30 September 2025) | Abstract and part of full text | Confirmed: up to 3.55% average improvement over the best baseline at near-equal inference cost; an early stop keeps the result at 49% of the inference count; one model at a time, no mixing of models. The paper's own figures for the gain disagree between sections (3.55% against 2.0% and 5.9%) |
